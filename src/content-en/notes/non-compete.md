## Research question

A non-compete trades economic compensation for the protection of trade secrets, yet the catch-all in Article 24 of the Labor Contract Law — "other persons with confidentiality obligations" — has been stretched by standard-form clauses in practice, expanding the obligated class from senior management down to grassroots workers who never knew any trade secret. Articles 13 to 15 of the Labor Dispute Judicial Interpretation (II), issued in 2025, respond to exactly this. The paper asks: how do these three provisions correct the over-extension as a matter of interpretation? They push judicial review of non-competes from "formal review" toward "substantive review" — what is the institutional significance of this turn, and where are its limits?

## Core argument

Articles 13 to 15 make three severing cuts, cutting off three taken-for-granted inferences: an agreement clause does not equal subject eligibility (Article 13: where the worker never came into contact with trade secrets, the non-compete clause does not take effect); an agreed scope does not equal a reasonable scope (Article 14: the part exceeding a reasonable proportion is void); an agreed term does not equal in-service effect (Article 15: the in-service non-compete rests on the duty of loyalty, is confined to executives and senior technical personnel, and is not conditioned on compensation). Taken together, the three provisions build a substantive-review framework that advances step by step from subject review to scope review to consideration review. The turn does not weaken trade-secret protection — only by excluding the low-quality clauses does the institution return to those who genuinely need confidentiality.

## Line of argument

1. **How the norms degenerated.** Articles 23 and 24 built the frame of "confidentiality obligation → written form → economic compensation" and the boundary of "three categories of personnel + a two-year cap"; the over-extension grew from three absences — no standard for definition, no procedure for conclusion, no consistency in adjudication. The consequences run three layers deep as well: the consideration is hollowed out, the job choice and livelihood of grassroots workers are suppressed, and the mobility of the talent market as a whole suffers.
2. **The substantive standard for subject eligibility (on Article 13).** The screening runs through the three elements of a trade secret (secrecy, value, confidentiality measures), with confidentiality at the source: without protective measures there is no trade secret, without a trade secret there is no confidentiality obligation, and the clause does not take effect. Job titles are separated from substantive judgment, paired with staged burdens of proof.
3. **Judicial control of the reasonable scope (on Articles 14 and 15).** The partial-voidity rule connects to Article 156 of the Civil Code; territory and duration must stay proportionate to the trade secret's shelf life; economic compensation carries the double function of consideration and ex ante screening; the in-service non-compete is confined to executives and senior technical personnel.
4. **From three severing cuts to a three-step review.** The three cuts are organized into a "three-step review method"; the three-stage test of the proportionality principle rebalances trade-secret protection against freedom of job choice; and the American experience is set against it — the FTC in 2024 briefly issued a rule banning non-competes nearly across the board, later set aside by the courts — to show how judicial review and administrative prohibition differ as routes.

## Cases and data

The sets of public material supporting the case for substantive review:

- The fourth batch of typical cases issued jointly by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security and the Supreme People's Court: a security guard's security plans and camera layouts were industry-common practice, not a trade secret — the non-compete was signed, yet the clause still does not take effect.
- The third batch of typical cases in the Sichuan-Chongqing region: a deputy head of the teaching-research group at a dance-training school, whose instructional videos had long been public and whose company never took protective measures — a specimen application of "no contact with trade secrets, no effect".
- A 2026 typical case from the Zhangjiakou Intermediate Court: the chief accountant held clients' tax accounts, passwords and filing data, and the company had access controls in place — all three elements present, a properly qualified subject. The three cases, positive and negative set against each other, show that a job title proves nothing.
- Guiding Case No. 190 (Wang Shan v. Wind Information): whether a competitive relationship exists turns on the actual business content, the customers served and the corresponding market — the business scope printed on the business license is no evidence.
- Xiong Hui and Wang Ruihong's statistics over 556 judgment documents from 2015 to 2019: 79% of the obligated subjects fell under "other persons with confidentiality obligations", 77% of those were grassroots posts such as sales, training instructors and line operators, and executives and senior technical personnel were only 21%.
- Starr, Prescott and Bishara's survey of roughly 11,500 workers across the United States: about four in ten respondents said a non-compete had influenced a career decision — even when unenforceable in court, the chilling effect persists.
- The Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security's Compliance Guidelines on Implementing Non-Competes, September 2025: economic compensation must be no less than 30% of the average wage over the 12 months before departure; where the term exceeds one year, the compensation ratio should not fall below 50%.

## Institutional recommendations

- Tier the obligated subjects by degree of exposure — core exposure, ordinary exposure, incidental exposure — each matched to a different intensity of restriction;
- Staged burdens of proof: the employer first proves plausible job exposure and the three trade-secret elements, the worker rebuts, and where the truth remains unproven the employer bears the adverse consequence, with courts empowered to investigate and verify on their own initiative;
- A disclosure duty at signing: at conclusion the employer issues a trade-secret exposure disclosure stating the scope of the secrets, the protective measures and the necessity, and a party that fails to do so bears the consequence of unproven evidence — moving the point of review up to the contracting stage;
- Sort the catch-all clause into defined types; leave non-competes in the new employment forms of the platform economy to legislation (the Platform Labor Rules and Algorithm Consultation Guidelines issued by four departments in March 2026 has not yet touched this).

The paper's conclusion reduces to one sentence: only those who genuinely need confidentiality should be asked to accept a non-compete.
