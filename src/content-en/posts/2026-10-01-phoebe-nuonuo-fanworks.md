Open Bilibili or Douyin: if your home feed has not been flooded by two cartoon little girls, then you genuinely have not been browsing the gaming section lately. The names Phoebe and Nuonuo have outgrown the range a pair of game characters should command: MAD remixes, hand-drawn art, edits, AI short dramas, desktop pets, home-made mini-games — the same pair of character settings done over and over, and still being done.

First get the characters straight; only then is there something to discuss. Phoebe is a character in the mobile game 《鸣潮》 (Wuthering Waves), launched with version 2.1 in February 2025; her official setting is an acolyte of the Order of the Deep. Nuonuo is not on the official roster: "Fu Nuonuo" is a player nickname for another character in the game, Phrolova, who shipped with version 2.5 in July 2025. The two barely cross paths in the game; it was fanworks that tied them into a pair of sisters: Phoebe bouncing around getting into trouble, Nuonuo trailing behind at her own pace cleaning up the mess.

This pairing was fan-written from day one, and the officials have never claimed it.

## The contrast was kneaded out by the players themselves

Retracing how they went viral, the most crucial step happened inside the fanwork community.

The official Phoebe is lively and skittish; the official Phrolova is quiet and melancholic — the two tonalities were always far apart. Fanworks amplified that gap into a comedy division of labor: Phoebe causes the trouble, Nuonuo handles the aftermath; Phoebe is loud and self-righteous, Nuonuo is wronged and pouting. Once the template stands, creators need not explain any background: a few glances, two frames of expression, and the audience instantly knows whom to scold and whom to pity. The crosstalk comedy pairing of the funny man and the straight man has been transplanted wholesale onto two game characters.

Handier still, the pairing is not picky about scripts. Snatching snacks, doing homework, slacking off at work, playing games online — any daily scene fits, and both personalities can carry it. The simpler the character settings, the wider the entrance for fanworks, and the more the works keep coming.

## From a few people's edits to a site-wide relay

Whether a topic escapes its circle does not depend on raw material alone; it depends on whether people in the circle are willing to keep building on it.

The Phoebe-and-Nuonuo fanworks grew from the bottom up: no official organizing, no unified campaign. At first someone just found a clip funny, cut it on a whim and posted it; the comment section picked up the joke and traded reaction images, patching the lore ever wilder. Later illustrators, editors and game makers joined in, and the same meme got worked into several versions. The topic even showed up in Bilibili's AI creation open competition.

The recent burst correlates directly with the falling barrier to creation. Someone wrote in a mini-game submission's title that building the two characters into a stage-clearing game took only one week. More and more people can use generative models and write a little code; making a short drama, a desktop pet, or a mini-game has gone from assembling a small squad to one person's week. Once output volume rose, the topic naturally overflowed the gaming section.

The curious part: I could barely find mainstream media coverage. The readable pieces come almost entirely from gaming-vertical self-media — the WeChat account BB Ji published one in June (reprinted by Huxiu) that calls the characters in such works "electronic daughters". A topic with play counts in the millions still sits inside its vertical circle to this day, which is exactly the evidence that its heat grew out of the community.

## What we are watching in all this is really our own days

At bottom, people are willing to spend time watching, remixing and spreading this because in the pile of silliness they have found a corner where the mental churning can be put down.

What one faces daily is work that never finishes and relationships that cannot be dodged, while these two little characters' biggest worry in a day is failing to claim a spot with sunlight, or being unable to tell whose snack pile is bigger. Their world is clean to the point of excess, and the people watching borrow it to rest a while. The phrase with the highest attendance rate in the comment section is "electronic daughter": rather than watching someone else's story, people are raising for themselves a home that cannot go wrong.

## Closing

Watching this crowd make fanworks, I keep thinking of the craft of making products: a character setting simple enough and forgiving enough, the barrier to creation pressed to the floor, the rest handed to the community — content grows on its own. Phoebe and Nuonuo happen to hold both. As for why everyone keeps at it without tiring, the answer is probably not complicated: in these two little characters there are no complicated people or matters to handle, and pure joy is enough.
