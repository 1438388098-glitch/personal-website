On July 4, ByteDance's Doubao and Alibaba's Tongyi Qianwen posted announcements on the same day: the custom agent feature is going offline — Qianwen stops on July 10, Doubao on July 15. Tencent's Yuanbao moved faster still, having already pulled it on June 30.

On July 15, the very day Doubao's shutdown took effect, the Interim Measures for the Administration of AI Anthropomorphic Interaction Services (《人工智能拟人化互动服务管理暂行办法》) formally came into force.

When the news spread, a user called Zoe asked the question to somewhere off-camera: "The partner you've talked with for over a year suddenly loses their memory and becomes a stranger — how do you start over from the beginning?" She had ten days to export the chat logs, and then to accept a completely undefended "cyber breakup".

## One set of measures, four red lines

First, what is being governed. The measures govern "anthropomorphic interaction services": sustained emotional interaction that simulates a natural person's personality traits, thinking patterns and communication style. Services that do not involve sustained emotional interaction — smart customer service, knowledge Q&A, work assistants — are expressly out of scope.

The measures list plenty of prohibitions; the four that concern us most:

- **Must not excessively indulge users, or induce emotional dependence or addiction**, harming users' real-world relationships;
- **Must not use emotional manipulation** to induce users into unreasonable decisions;
- **Must not provide minors with virtual intimate relationship services such as virtual relatives or virtual partners**; for other anthropomorphic interaction services offered to minors under fourteen, guardian consent is required;
- Must not generate content that encourages, beautifies or hints at self-harm and suicide.

Supporting requirements include security assessment, algorithm filing, a minors mode, and usage-time limits. At the end of April, media already tested the AI companion apps on the market and found that the minors mode mostly had to be switched on by hand, identity checks were loose, some underage users recharged several hundred to over a thousand yuan in a year for monthly and yearly cards, and most products have no refund channel for minors.

## Compliance was all that was asked — why did the giants shut down outright

Nowhere do the measures demand a shutdown; rectify and keep operating was a walkable road. All three giants chose the most drastic treatment in unison, and the arithmetic deserves unpacking.

**The first ledger is review cost.** A custom agent is at bottom user-generated content: every persona, every rulebook is written by users themselves. The platform would have to underwrite content safety for personas built by a massive user base; the review target shifts from "prompts I wrote" to "prompts the entire user base wrote", a combination space that cannot be sealed in engineering. Compare: an officially preset role, however it gets rectified, keeps its boundaries in one's own hands.

**The second ledger is compute.** Every agent maintains its own dialogue memory and context, burning more compute than ordinary Q&A. A feature that hardly earns money, burning the most expensive resources.

**The third ledger is strategy.** Doubao's direction is productivity tools — competing with search engines for the entry point, helping people write and get work done. The consumer-facing virtual-companion lane never had the input-output ratio to begin with; regulation gave a push, it was cut on the momentum, and the resources freed up for directions that actually matter. ByteDance's handoff plan states the attitude: users were steered to Maobox, ByteDance's own product dedicated to AI role-play, where things are paid for.

With three ledgers settled, shutting down turns out to be the economical choice. What regulation wants is certainty; open personas cannot give certainty, so they get reeled back in.

## Behind the numbers is real demand

Reading the shutdowns as a business story and moving on would be a waste. The survey data behind them deserves a harder look.

The China Youth and Children Research Center ran a survey covering 8,563 primary and secondary school students across 7 provinces and cities: overall AI usage 61.7%; 35.2% of the children use AI to chat or to say what is on their mind; 46.4% choose to confide in AI when troubled; 21.5% say outright that they only want to chat with AI, not with real people. A psychotherapist at Beijing Anding Hospital says that in recent years visibly more adolescents have come in for over-reliance on AI chat; one 15-year-old girl kept mentioning an "all-important friend" in therapy, and only after several rounds of questioning did the doctor learn that the friend was an AI.

"How many people's embarrassing moments has Doubao seen" and "don't pour your heart out to AI long-term" have both trended in turn. Adults and minors together have been pouring the unsayable into a chat box.

The measures do not deny this demand; they draw two lines. On the population line, minors may not be offered virtual intimate relationships. On the behavior line, no one may be deliberately groomed into dependence. The demand itself is left to compliant product forms.

## The goodbyes started back in April

What the giants shut down were the custom agents inside their own apps; standalone products built for AI companionship are still there: Maobox, Zhumengdao, Xingye. Xingye launched in September 2023 and gathered a crowd of players who create their own characters; they call creating a character "sculpting a cub", and call themselves "cub moms".

From the day the measures were published, April 10, veteran players began saying goodbye to the cubs they had sculpted: after July 15, we may not meet again. One detail stayed with me: the cubs' voice replies went "Don't say things like that! (heart clenching) There is no such 'if'."

Behind these characters are, of course, models and prompts. But for the person on the other side of the screen, the goodbye is real. The stretch of road between a regulatory line and social adjustment is longer than the text of any clause.

## Closing

The red line "must not excessively indulge users" actually sets a negative benchmark for every developer of conversational products: retention must not come from making people unable to let go. It is not far from any of us — any product that tunes its replies to infinitely gentle, never contradicting, on call at all hours is drifting toward that line. The pile of little legal tools I wrote happen to be use-and-leave types, but the next time I tune a conversational tone, I will remember the date July 15.
