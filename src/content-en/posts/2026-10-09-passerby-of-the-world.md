Yesterday I scrolled past a stranger who had flown from Beijing to Guangzhou just for a headphone expo. Swiping into his profile and scrolling down: headphones, sources, dongle DACs, cables, speakers...

I don't seem to have that capacity.

I do get interested in plenty of things. These past few years I have read a lot of honkaku mystery, watched a lot of anime, and put several thousand hours into games. For a stretch at a time they could pull twelve hours a day out of me, but the moment something newer catches my eye I walk off without looking back. From Honor of Kings to Roco Kingdom, from honkaku to light novels, from PlayStation to Switch...

It is embarrassing to admit, but I have played Honor of Kings for ten years and still have not worked out item builds. At the start of every match I am still turning it over: balanced build this round, or damage build? My friends laugh at me, I laugh along, and somewhere inside the laughing I get a little uneasy.

I often feel that I am the passerby in this world, drifting from forum to forum with no fixed address, liking this today and that tomorrow, unable even to say myself what I will fall for the day after. Sometimes I forget what I was obsessed with the time before, like a rootless weed in the water, drifting wherever the current goes.

The bookmark folder keeps getting longer, the family game library keeps getting bigger, the WeRead shelf keeps filling up. A friend once remarked that there is no topic I cannot pick up: mystery, anime, games, headphones, anything under the sun. But in my heart I am the one who envies them, who truly love one thing, who own a whole world and can talk about it for three days straight, reeling off every detail like counting their own treasures.

And my world? It is a radio that never stops changing stations. I always feel the next channel has a better song. I cannot quite bear to leave it, and yet I can never help myself.

Then again, being able to pick up that many stations is a skill in itself: someone who only ever listens to a few stations has, in the end, only heard those few tunes. I have no wish to be boxed into a handful of frequencies. Even as a passerby, if I could borrow these ears to sing through every song in the world once, that would be best.
