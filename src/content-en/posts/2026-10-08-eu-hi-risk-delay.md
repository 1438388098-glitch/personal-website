On July 31 the European Commission published its communiqué: from August 2, the AI Act's application widens. I wrote about [that effective list](/en/blog/2026-08-05-eu-ai-act-transparency/) three months ago — chatbots must announce themselves, deepfakes must be labeled, providers of general-purpose models come under the EU AI Office. Those transparency rules landed on schedule.

The second half of the communiqué says something else: the high-risk obligations that were due the same day have been postponed.

## The timeline of a delay

The AI Act entered into force in August 2024, applying in phases by risk level, with most obligations due on August 2, 2026 — high-risk AI systems included. The turn came on November 19, 2025, when the Commission tabled a 65-page formal proposal to delay the high-risk provisions by at least a year, bundled with a broader simplification package. 127 civil-society organizations objected publicly, calling it the largest rollback of digital rights in the EU's history.

On March 26, 2026, the European Parliament voted the simplification through, and the dates settled. The July 31 communiqué fixed two of them in official language: rules for high-social-risk uses — hiring, education, law enforcement, migration management (Annex III) — apply from December 2, 2027; rules for high-risk systems embedded in regulated products such as medical devices and machinery (Annex I) apply from August 2, 2028. A year and a half later than the original timetable.

## One train, two directions

One rider on this revision deserves its own look: the Parliament attached a ban on "nudification" applications — using AI to turn photos of real people into nudes without consent is now, for the first time, written into legislation. After the wave of Grok-generated abuse, the ban reached political consensus in May 2026 and landed on this very train.

The direction of this revision, then, is not one-way: high-risk obligations loosened, red lines on specific abuses tightened. The political economy of regulation shows its true shape here — the same document that relieves industry also owes the public an answer.

## For anyone shipping products

Recalculate the compliance calendar. Teams building tools for education, hiring or law enforcement gain a year and a half of runway on the high-risk obligations. But the transparency duties were not postponed: since August 2, an interactive AI that fails to announce itself is in violation in the EU, and that binds every chat product with EU users. Keep two dates straight: December 2, 2027 for Annex III; August 2, 2028 for Annex I.

The timetable is the policy. 16 months of delay is not a technical adjustment; it is the EU's formal answer to the fight between digital rights and industrial competitiveness: the right to know arrives on schedule, the high-risk obligations wait.
